How RealStack works

From first call to funded loan, on one platform.

You run the deal in RealStack Ops. Your borrower plans in Mortgage Blueprint. Here is what each of you sees at every step.

You, in OpsYour borrower, in Blueprint
STAGE 01

The lead arrives

You, in Ops

Arive files, a quick New lead, or a homebuyer's Get Pre-Approved request all land on your Leads tab, with Gmail history attached.

Your borrower, in Blueprint

Finds you through a referral, or plays with the free Blueprint calculator and taps Get Pre-Approved.

STAGE 02

You build the Blueprint

You, in Ops

Import the file from Arive and build two or three real options in minutes. Your name, phone and NMLS fill in.

Your borrower, in Blueprint

Nothing to do yet. Their Blueprint is being built with their real numbers, ready the moment they open it.

STAGE 03

You send it

You, in Ops

One email from your own Gmail with an Open my Blueprint button, or a link you paste anywhere.

Your borrower, in Blueprint

Gets "Your personalized mortgage Blueprint" and taps the button.

STAGE 04

They sign in

You, in Ops

Manage access shows the moment they first sign in. You decide who else can open it and what they can change.

Your borrower, in Blueprint

Signs in with Google or a one-time code. Only the emails on their file can open it.

STAGE 05

You work it together

You, in Ops

Blueprint Activity shows every change. Lock income and assets once you have checked the documents.

Your borrower, in Blueprint

Changes price, down payment and rate, compares options, and adds a spouse or agent.

STAGE 06

Application to close

You, in Ops

The file moves to Live Loans. Timeline emails and stage-based follow-ups go out from your Gmail.

Your borrower, in Blueprint

Gets clear updates at every milestone, from the same loan officer they have been talking to.

STAGE 07

After closing

You, in Ops

The closing checklist, then Refi Hunter flags them when rates would save them money.

Your borrower, in Blueprint

Keeps their Blueprint, and hears from you when a refinance actually makes sense.
